# Executive Summary

NMS has a potentially valuable story: Johannesburg herbal roots repeatedly traced in legacy material to 1934, a historically broad botanical catalogue, and signs of past pharmacy and wholesale infrastructure. The same project record also shows why a rapid cosmetic relaunch would be unsafe. The present legal entity and ownership are not established; product, formula, licence, price, stock and claims data are fragmented; historical materials contain direct and implied high-risk therapeutic claims; brand ownership and hierarchy are unresolved; and the draft product master places every one of its 247 tracked rows on Hold.

**Proposal.** JB3AI recommends an approximately 18-month, stage-gated transformation rather than an immediate full-catalogue relaunch. The first objective is to establish current corporate, product and compliance truth. The second is to select three to five hero products through mandatory risk screening and a weighted portfolio scorecard. The third is to create a modular masterbrand, an accountable information system and a controlled service platform. Only then should NMS pilot in Gauteng through approved direct-to-consumer activity, one specialist ecommerce partner and a limited independent-pharmacy cohort. Scale into further provinces, chains or range extensions should depend on evidence of safety, repeat purchase, gross contribution, supply resilience and service performance.

SAHPRA’s complementary-medicines framework places health supplements within Category D and limits them to applicable low-risk substances, doses and indications, while continued sale is linked to registration-call-up and licensing requirements and other relevant provisions.[1][2][5] SAHPRA guidance makes claims formulation-, dose-, population-, evidence- and warning-specific; its quality guidance expects structured evidence covering specifications, methods, batch analysis, stability, manufacturing sites, packaging, labelling and release controls.[3][4] This makes claims governance and product-level quality evidence foundational operating systems, not late-stage copy checks.

The commercial and customer experience should be equally controlled. A mobile-first, low-data website should begin as an approved catalogue, education, stockist, trade and service environment. Checkout is a later operational gate. Shopify is a viable platform option, but it has not been selected and should be compared with alternatives against PIM integration, payments, inventory, shipping, accessibility, privacy, analytics, governance, total cost and support. Calls, chatbot, email, WhatsApp and social interactions should feed one CRM case model with immediate routes for complaints, product-quality concerns, adverse events and privacy requests.

The proposed brand position is **“Johannesburg herbal heritage, made accountable”** as an internal strategy line. The consumer promise is controlled transparency: clearly named ingredients, understandable product information, traceable quality ownership, appropriate warnings and claims that remain inside approved evidence. Three identity themes and three logo territories should be developed and tested. “Modern Botanical” is the recommended starting hypothesis, not a predetermined design outcome.

No final prices are included. The source materials contain incompatible commercial scenarios, currencies and scope structures that must not be added together. The Board should approve a bottom-up, stage-gated costing method after Gate A establishes the true entity, portfolio, compliance scope and implementation requirements.

The immediate executive requirement is therefore not approval to “launch a new website.” It is approval to establish programme authority, preserve the archive, verify the company and active portfolio, appoint accountable regulatory and operational owners, quarantine historical claims, commission three identity routes, evaluate platform options and fund the evidence needed for Gates A and B.

# 1. Brand and History

## 1.1 The Supported Heritage

**Verified project fact.** Multiple legacy and strategic records describe NMS as having Johannesburg or South African herbal roots traced to **1934**. The supplied evidence does not, however, prove uninterrupted legal succession from 1934 to the current operating entity. The correct working language is therefore **“roots traced to 1934 in Johannesburg,” subject to documentary and legal approval**. “Established 1934,” “since 1934” and other continuity claims remain on Hold.

Historical materials describe more than 150 simplex or raw herbs alongside remedies, medicinal teas and compounds, topicals, hair, scalp and oral products, tissue salts, nutrition products, beverages, substitutes and imported or distributed ranges. They also contain internal codes, barcodes, NAPPI fields, case-pack logic, wholesale references and names associated with pharmacy and health-shop channels. These records demonstrate archival breadth and past commercial structure; they do not prove current products, registrations, listings, relationships, prices or rights.

Legacy brand names include NMS/Natural Medicinal Services, Nature’s Own, NU Organic, Lexan, Hermes and other distributed ranges. Present ownership, trademark position and intended hierarchy are not verified. Swiss distribution is variously dated to 1970 or 1979, while a related name appears as both Kernosan and Kennosan. Both points remain archival and unsuitable for public use until resolved.

## 1.2 Heritage Policy

**Proposal.** Preserve every legacy brochure, price list, photograph, product sheet and trade reference in a controlled Heritage Archive. Nothing should be deleted, but archive status must be visibly separated from current approval. Public history should be limited to evidence that legal counsel and the designated NMS fact owner have approved.

The 1934 device should be modular and removable from identity, packaging and templates. This protects the brand if continuity evidence remains insufficient and prevents the visual system from becoming dependent on an unverified assertion. Third-party retailer names, imported-brand marks and past distribution statements must not be used as endorsements without current authority.

**Decision.** Leadership must select one of three treatments: verified “Established 1934,” qualified “Roots traced to 1934,” or no date claim. JB3AI recommends the qualified route only if legal review accepts the available evidence.

# 2. Current State

## 2.1 Corporate and Operating Truth

**Verified project fact.** Candidate company registration references include 2007/017961/07, 1980/008685/07 and an incomplete 2011 number. Candidate names include “Natural Medicinal Service,” “Natural Medicinal Services (Pty) Ltd” and “Natural Medicinal Services TVL. (Pty) Ltd.” Ownership references alternate between “Mvelo” and “Myelo Pharmaceuticals Group,” accompanied by an undated “recent acquisition.” None may be treated as authoritative before CIPC, shareholder and transaction-record diligence.

Addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, domains, product codes, product names, VAT calculations, returns terms and availability statements also conflict. As a result, no legacy document should be used as a current company fact sheet or uploaded directly into a website.

## 2.2 Portfolio, Quality and Claims

**Verified project fact.** The working product master dated 16 August 2026 tracks 247 rows: 27 core historical price-list rows, 71 additional legacy products or variants, and 149 crude-herb lines. Every row is on Hold or “on enquiry—hold until identity, safety and regulatory review.” None of the eight packaging and quality release checks has started.

Historical prices derive from a price list effective 1 May 2017 and are not current commercial data. Newer file metadata sometimes surrounds reproduced 2017 content, so metadata cannot establish currentness. Product information remains dispersed among brochures, PDFs, price lists and draft files rather than one approved source containing formula, specification, classification, claims, labels, stock, cost, price, margin, logistics, digital assets and ownership.

Legacy copy includes treatment, cure, detoxification, blood-pressure, diabetes, infection, pregnancy, slimming and other high-risk outcomes. Some records simultaneously acknowledge toxicity, carcinogenicity, interactions and the inability of herbs to cure ailments. This contradiction is both a safety issue and a brand credibility issue.

**Hold.** All legacy product descriptions, claims, testimonials, prices, barcodes, NAPPI references, formulas, warnings, retailer references and availability statements are quarantined. No item should migrate to packaging, the website, a chatbot, social media or sales materials without current product-specific approval.

## 2.3 Brand, Digital and Service Readiness

The brand architecture remains unresolved. “Nature’s Own” may have recognition, but ownership, protectability, punctuation, comprehension and the risk of implying “natural equals harmless” have not been tested. Ten intended asset classes are documented—primary and wordmark-only marks, monograms, endorsements, avatar, signature, header and icon system—but all remain to be designed. The “NMS Standard” version 0.9 token set is a challenger concept, not an approved standard.

Website and shop terms are stale or contradictory, including return windows. The record does not show complete readiness for live stock, current price and VAT, payment, courier, privacy, fulfilment, returns, complaints, adverse events or support. The current state is therefore an archive and control challenge before it is a design challenge.

# 3. Future Implementation and Gates

## 3.1 Programme Design

**Proposal.** The programme should use short delivery sprints inside an approximately 18-month gated transformation. A “90-day” workstream may accelerate internal validation, design and build activity; it must not be interpreted as automatic permission for a public launch.

| Phase | Indicative timing | Principal outputs | Approval gate |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Mobilise and preserve | Week 0–1 | Charter, named owners, data-room structure, archive freeze, access rules, issue and decision logs | Mobilisation acceptance |
| Corporate, product and compliance truth | Weeks 1–6 | Verified company fact sheet, ownership and signatories, licence/manufacturing map, active-product universe, claims red list, quality-gap assessment | **Gate A — Truth approved** |
| Market and commercial validation | Weeks 2–10 | Customer and channel research, sales/repeat baseline, current SKU economics, supply review, scored hero candidates | **Gate B — 3–5 hero candidates approved** |
| Brand, architecture and experience | Weeks 7–14 | Name and trademark work, positioning, architecture, voice, three themes, three logo routes, packaging hierarchy, service blueprint | Strategy approval |
| Identity, packaging and platform build | Weeks 12–26 | Approved identity, hero packaging, governed PIM/CMS, catalogue, CRM, service channels, policies, analytics, SOPs and UAT | **Gate C — Launch readiness approved** |
| Gauteng pilot | Approximately months 7–9 | Controlled NMS DTC where ready, one specialist ecommerce partner, 15–30 independent pharmacies, safety and economics monitoring | **Gate D — Pilot evidence accepted** |
| Scale what works | Months 10–18 | Selective KZN and Western Cape expansion, chain submissions, range extensions and optimisation | Board investment decision |

## 3.2 Gate Requirements

### Gate A — Truth Approved

Gate A requires the verified contracting and operating entity, registration and ownership statement; authorised signatories; approved legal particulars and contacts; licence and manufacturing map; approved company facts; active-product universe; and named accountable owners. No public identity rollout or retailer presentation should precede this gate.

### Gate B — Portfolio Approved

Gate B requires three to five hero candidates selected through the exact eight-factor scorecard in Section 14, with evidence confidence recorded separately. Any unresolved prohibited claim, formula identity issue, critical warning gap, safety concern, licence gap or unstable supply route disqualifies a product before commercial scoring.

### Gate C — Launch Readiness Approved

Gate C requires approved master records, labels, claims, directions and warnings; release-ready stock; current price/VAT and logistics; all eight release checks; tested fulfilment and returns; approved privacy and commerce policies; controlled publishing workflow; accessibility and low-data performance; analytics and consent testing; CRM and chatbot UAT; adverse-event escalation; security and incident-response evidence; and signed executive, regulatory and quality approval.

### Gate D — Pilot Evidence Accepted

Gate D reviews sell-through, trial-to-repeat, repeat interval, gross contribution, cash conversion, fill rate, on-time-in-full delivery, stockouts, returns, complaints, adverse events, service levels, digital conversion and retailer reorder evidence. Scale is evidence-led, not calendar-led. Numerical thresholds should be approved only after baseline economics and channel terms are known.

## 3.3 Data Governance

The controlled data room should contain Corporate Legal & Succession; Regulatory & Licensing; IP & Brand; Commercial & Financial; Digital, IT & Policies; and Heritage Archive folders, with a separate operating PIM workspace where required. Each approved record must identify owner, approver, version, source evidence, effective date, review date and superseded version.

The PIM or controlled product master—not a brochure, Shopify page, chatbot or staff memory—must be the source for every approved product title, formula, claim, warning, price, stock state, pack attribute and digital asset. Role-based workflow, version history and rollback are non-negotiable controls.

# 4. New Brand

## 4.1 Position and Promise

**Proposal.** Use **“Johannesburg herbal heritage, made accountable”** as the internal positioning line. It connects the heritage opportunity with present-day proof without promising efficacy or uninterrupted corporate history. It should not become consumer copy until legal, regulatory and comprehension review is complete.

The proposed promise is **controlled transparency**: botanical self-care that is understandable, inspectable and responsible. Proof should include common and botanical ingredient names; plant part where relevant; controlled manufacturing and batch traceability; clear dosage form, directions and warnings; visible quality ownership; accessible customer escalation; and claims bounded by approved information and accepted evidence.

The brand voice should be calm, plain, precise, respectful and non-alarmist. It should avoid cure language, mystical certainty, unsupported superlatives, “detox” shorthand, implied clinical endorsement and any suggestion that natural products are harmless or can replace prescribed treatment.

## 4.2 Proposed Architecture

| Layer | Proposed role | Control condition |
|---|---|---|
| NMS / Natural Medicinal Services | Corporate masterbrand and accountable organisation | Legal name and trademark position approved |
| NMS Quality Mark | Controlled endorsement where NMS can substantiate its role | Exact wording, quality ownership and licence implications approved |
| Consumer-facing range | Nature’s Own only if legally available and understood; otherwise a replacement name | Trademark, ownership and comprehension testing passed |
| Need-state families | Navigation such as Daily Balance, Digestive Comfort, Calm & Rest, Seasonal Support, Mobility and Topical Care | Names reviewed as claims and supported by portfolio logic |
| Product descriptor | Clear product identity, dosage form, botanical identity, quantity, directions and warnings | Approved master record governs |
| Distributed brands | Explicitly separated third-party or distributed ranges | Rights and roles documented |

This endorsed masterbrand hypothesis keeps the consumer-name layer replaceable, the heritage date detachable and selection-safety information independent from logo size. It allows NMS to build authority without embedding unresolved legal or historical claims in every pack.

**Decision.** Approve NMS as the corporate masterbrand hypothesis, determine whether a consumer sub-brand is needed, and commission diligence on Nature’s Own before any final naming investment.

# 5. New Look and Feel and Rationale

The visual system should make accountability visible. Rather than imitating a traditional apothecary or a clinical laboratory, it should combine botanical warmth with disciplined information design. The customer must be able to recognise the brand, identify the exact product, understand its form and quantity, find directions and warnings, and know where to ask for help.

**Proposal.** Use clear typographic hierarchy, generous but efficient spacing, high contrast, restrained botanical imagery, structured ingredient information and a limited palette. Packaging should distinguish brand, family and product without creating a wall of decorative green. Photography should show ingredients and products accurately, avoid exaggerated transformation imagery, and remain consistent with approved claims.

The design rationale is practical:

| Design choice | Rationale | Required test |
|---|---|---|
| Wordmark-led identity | Supports corporate authority and avoids dependence on a generic botanical icon | Recognition, trademark and small-size tests |
| Modular heritage element | Preserves heritage value without hard-wiring an unverified date | Legal approval and removal test |
| Information-first packs | Reduces selection and use errors | Shelf, comprehension and regulatory review |
| Warm neutral backgrounds | Improves readability and avoids undifferentiated “green wellness” presentation | Contrast, substrate and photography tests |
| One controlled accent | Creates range navigation without implying risk or efficacy | Colour-vision and monochrome tests |
| Accessible digital components | Supports mobile, low-data and assistive-technology use | WCAG-informed audit and device testing |
| Consistent icon language | Helps scanning without replacing mandatory text | Comprehension and production tests |

Product name, dosage form, ingredient identity, quantity, directions and warnings must never be reduced merely to enlarge the logo. Every concept should be evaluated in black and white first, then on mobile, shelf, packaging, technical documents, social avatars, office print and low-cost production.

# 6. Marketing Plan: Website and Social

**Proposal.** Marketing should create trustworthy discovery and repeatable customer relationships before maximising reach. Priority audiences are value-conscious Gauteng households, ingredient-aware online shoppers, adults using herbs alongside prescribed medicines, independent pharmacists and health-shop buyers, and practitioner or raw-herb buyers where the product and channel are appropriate.

The initial channel order should be owned website and search, service-led CRM/email, WhatsApp care, independent pharmacy support, one specialist ecommerce partner, selective practitioner/trade outreach, and tightly controlled paid search and social experiments. Testimonials require source retention, consent, authenticity checks and compliance review. NMS must not repost an unapproved disease or outcome claim simply because a customer made it. Influencers require contracts, disclosure obligations, prohibited-claims training, approved briefs and pre-publication review where possible. Personal health information posted publicly should be hidden or moved to a controlled private route.

# 7. Products and Rebranding

## 7.1 Portfolio Control

The 247-row draft master is the best current inventory-control structure but is not a saleable catalogue. Historical groups include finished oral remedies, teas and compounds, raw herbs, topicals, hair/scalp/oral products, nutrition and beverage products, tissue salts and imported ranges. Every item remains non-publishable until the physical product, controlled specification, classification, licence route, label, commercial data, logistics and assets are approved.

## 7.2 Triage Before Scoring

| Triage status | Meaning | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| **Red** | Disease/cure claim, uncertain identity, material safety concern, licence gap, unstable manufacture or prohibited name/position | Quarantine; substantiate, reformulate, rename or retire |
| **Amber** | Potentially viable but evidence, label, claim, economics, supply or data is incomplete | Remediate and reassess |
| **Green candidate** | Lower-risk proposition with a verified record, stable supply and plausible economics | Eligible for weighted scoring; not automatically approved |

**Proposal.** Select only three to five hero products for the Gauteng pilot. Increase to six to ten only after proof of compliant repeat demand, gross contribution and resilient supply.

## 7.3 Rebranding Standard

Rebranding is not a label reskin. Every hero product needs a controlled record containing its legal product name; brand and range; dosage form and quantity; full formulation; common and scientific names; relevant plant part; allergens, interactions and warnings; directions; storage and shelf life; lot and expiry method; regulatory classification; licence and responsible-person information; permitted claims; barcode and NAPPI status; current cost, price, VAT, margin and MOQ; packaging and logistics; returns disposition; and approved imagery, metadata and alt text.

The crude-herb list includes spelling and botanical-name issues, including variants of *Pimpinella anisum*, *Dioscorea villosa* and *Achillea millefolium*. A qualified botanist, pharmacognosist or appropriately competent specialist must validate taxonomy and plant parts before approval.

Packaging should use one hierarchy across bottles, cartons, pouches and cases. Safety and selection information outranks decorative treatment. Production proofing must cover real substrates, print tolerances, barcodes, minimum type, colour shifts, batch and expiry coding, tamper evidence where applicable, and warehouse or retail handling.

# 8. Logo Options and Selection Method

## 8.1 Three Logo Territories

| Option | Concept | Strategic value | Principal risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The Verified Root** | Rounded provenance symbol with restrained geometric roots and an upward botanical gesture, paired with a clear NMS wordmark | Connects traceability, origin and living heritage | Can become another generic leaf badge |
| **2. The NMS Standard** | Custom wordmark or boxed N–M–S monogram inspired by label panels, records and controlled containers | Signals systems, quality and institutional confidence | Can feel rigid or imply unearned certification |
| **3. Botanical Ledger** | Typographic or ledger-led identity centred on named evidence, measurement and information architecture rather than a plant icon | Creates a distinctive, compliance-neutral challenger | May require stronger warmth and consumer explanation |

A provenance ring may be developed as a supporting heritage device, not as an automatic fourth logo route. The existing NMS Standard version 0.9 is a challenger input only.

## 8.2 Weighted Selection Method

| Criterion | Weight | Pass requirement |
|---|---:|---|
| Trust and authority | 20% | Credible without suggesting clinical or regulatory approval |
| Distinctiveness | 15% | Recognisable and ownable without category clichés |
| Legibility and scale | 15% | Clear at approximately 24 px and 8 mm, including monochrome |
| Brand-architecture fit | 15% | Works as masterbrand, endorsement and modular system |
| Compliance neutrality | 15% | No cure, guaranteed efficacy, harmlessness or approval implication; failure disqualifies |
| Digital and low-data performance | 10% | Efficient SVG, favicon/avatar clarity and accessible contrast |
| Production versatility | 10% | Works in one colour, office print, thermal print, emboss and foil |

The selection process should move through legal screening, black-and-white review, staff and audience comprehension, shelf and mobile simulation, small-size and production tests, accessibility review and executive sign-off. Personal preference should be recorded only after mandatory compliance, legal and functional tests.

The final identity package should include the ten planned asset classes: primary wordmark, wordmark-only, boxed monogram, micro monogram, quality endorsement, corporate endorsement, social avatar, email signature, document header and a 24-unit information-icon system. Formats should include suitable vector, print and web outputs, with a controlled asset register.

Avoid a generic leaf in a circle, mortar and pestle, medical cross or caduceus, primary checkmark, hands holding a leaf, DNA/molecule/flask imagery, green gradients, gloss, faux-medical shields, a 1934 crest and overly detailed root bundles.

# 10. SAHPRA, Regulation and Compliance

## 10.1 Regulatory Baseline

SAHPRA describes Category D complementary medicines as including discipline-specific medicines and health supplements. Health supplements are limited to applicable low-risk substances, dosage ranges and indications in the guidance; low-risk framing includes general health enhancement without disease reference, health maintenance and relief of minor symptoms not linked to a disease or disorder.[1][2] Continued sale is connected to applicable registration-call-up requirements, relevant section 22C licensing, section 20 and specified regulations and provisions.[1][5]

Classification and lawful treatment are product-specific. Composition, daily dose, intended use, target population, claims, scheduling, registration status, licence scope, manufacturing arrangements and label content must be assessed by NMS’s appointed regulatory specialists. This proposal is not a classification decision.

SAHPRA’s health-supplement guidance includes allowable claim wording, ingredient-specific dose parameters, mandatory warnings, treatment of single- and multiple-substance formulations, and safety and efficacy requirements.[3] Claims must therefore be controlled against the actual product record, not improvised as brand copy. SAHPRA’s quality guidance expects structured administrative and technical material, including specifications, analytical procedures and validation, stability-indicating methods where relevant, batch analysis, certificates of analysis, manufacturing-site information, packaging, labelling and release controls.[4]

## 10.2 Claims Governance

**Hold.** Disease treatment, cure, detoxification, blood-pressure, diabetes, infection, pregnancy, slimming and similar legacy outcome language remains quarantined. Product names can themselves communicate claims and require review.

Each SKU should have an approved claims matrix containing exact permitted wording, prohibited terms, required qualifiers, mandatory warnings, evidence links, responsible approver, date, approved channels and review date. The same matrix must govern packaging, retailer sheets, website, search metadata, chatbot, calls, WhatsApp, email, social, creators and testimonials.

A Responsible Pharmacist or the legally appropriate authorised professional should approve master SKU records, labels, advertisements, educational content and scripts. The exact statutory role must be confirmed for each product and licence arrangement.

## 10.3 Quality, Safety and Traceability

A released product requires controlled formulation and botanical identity; qualified suppliers; approved specifications and certificates; manufacturing and batch records; a shelf-life and stability basis; adulteration and contamination controls; storage requirements; authorised release; complaint and recall routes; and lot-level traceability. SAHPRA’s quality guidance describes final-product certificates of analysis and additional local release expectations for imported batches unless exempted.[4]

Customer-facing information must cover directions, dosage, contraindications, interactions, allergens, storage, shelf life, pregnancy or breastfeeding cautions where applicable, and when to seek qualified help. NMS communications must respect prescribed medicines and must not encourage a customer to stop or alter treatment without professional advice.

Indigenous or conservation-sensitive sourcing requires species confirmation, applicable permits, sustainability and supplier-rights checks, provenance evidence and benefit-sharing review. Provenance marketing should wait until the evidence chain is complete.

## 10.4 POPIA and Direct Marketing

The Information Regulator’s direct-marketing guidance treats telephone, email, SMS, push notifications, Instagram or LinkedIn direct messages and cookies as electronic direct-marketing channels.[6][7] Marketing to a non-customer requires consent; a person may be approached once to request consent if that person has not previously withheld it. Existing-customer marketing is limited to the responsible party’s own similar products or services where the details were collected in a sale context, with a free, simple objection opportunity at collection and in every message. Objections must stop future marketing, and NMS should maintain a suppression record.[7]

Operational controls should include defined lawful purpose, minimum necessary data, consent where required, clear notices, secure access, retention and deletion rules, processor agreements, data-subject request routes and breach response. Health-related information deserves heightened restriction. Promotional profiling based on inferred health conditions should not occur without specialist privacy assessment.

## 10.5 CPA, ECTA and Ecommerce

The Consumer Protection Act establishes a framework intended to improve consumer information and prohibit unfair marketing and business practices.[8] The Electronic Communications and Transactions Act regulates and facilitates electronic communications and transactions.[9] NMS’s digital store should therefore present clear seller identity and contact information, accurate product and price disclosures, delivery and return terms, transaction records, consent and privacy controls, and marketing claims that remain inside the approved evidence envelope.[8][9]

Legal counsel must translate these requirements into current NMS terms, cancellation and return processes, refunds, delivery commitments, records and notices. Historic seven- and fourteen-day references are conflicting and cannot be reused as policy.

## 10.6 Safety and Release Controls

The customer-service SOP must distinguish a general enquiry, order issue, complaint, product-quality complaint, adverse event and urgent safety concern. Staff should capture the minimum necessary information, time-stamp and preserve the source, escalate to trained owners, meet applicable reporting obligations and close the loop.

A SKU becomes publishable or orderable only when all eight release checks are green:

| Release check | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Physical stock verified | Reconciled quantity, condition, location and lot information |
| Controlled specification approved | Current formula, identity, specification and responsible approval |
| Regulatory category approved | Documented classification, licence and claim route |
| Barcode/NAPPI validated | Current ownership, check digit, pack mapping and status |
| Artwork approved | Regulatory, quality, legal, brand and production sign-off |
| Commercial data approved | Current cost, ZAR price, VAT, margin, MOQ and channel terms |
| Logistics approved | Dimensions, mass, storage, shelf life, case pack, courier and returns route |
| Digital assets approved | Product copy, images, metadata, alt text and channel permissions |

A historical NAPPI or barcode field does not prove current validity, ownership or reimbursement status.

# 11. Customer Engagement

## 11.1 One Case Model Across All Channels

**Proposal.** Calls, call bookings, chatbot, email, web forms, WhatsApp and social direct messages should create or update one governed CRM case. # 14. Weighted Product Criteria

The eight criteria below are the controlling portfolio model and total 100%. Their names are retained exactly as requested. Mandatory disqualifiers and the eight release checks apply before and after scoring; a commercially attractive product cannot score its way around a legal, safety, identity or manufacturing failure.

| Criterion | Weight | What must be assessed | Minimum evidence |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Regulatory & claims readiness** | **25%** | Classification, licence fit, formula identity, permitted claims, warnings, label feasibility and evidence | Approved master and claims matrix; no unresolved Red issue |
| **Existing demand/repeat potential** | **15%** | Current sales, active enquiries, retailer interest, repurchase interval and problem relevance | Current sales/cohort evidence where available; research and pilot data; archive clearly separated |
| **Gross contribution** | **15%** | Net revenue after product and direct channel costs, including VAT, discount, payment, fulfilment, delivery support, returns, trade margin and promotion | Current costed product/pack, approved price architecture and channel economics |
| **Supply & quality resilience** | **15%** | Supplier continuity, botanical identity, specification, yield, lead time, MOQ, consistency, stability, contamination control and alternatives | Approved specification, qualified suppliers, quality history and continuity plan |
| **Brand differentiation** | **10%** | Credible, distinctive customer value without prohibited claims; provenance and format advantage | Competitor review, comprehension testing and defensible story |
| **Retail operability** | **10%** | Shelf clarity, pack, barcode/NAPPI, case pack, shelf life, margin, fulfilment, replenishment, returns and training burden | Approved packaging/logistics master and buyer-economics test |
| **Digital discoverability** | **5%** | Search demand, clear compliant language, structured data, content potential and mobile comprehension | Intent research and compliant metadata/content route |
| **Cross-sell/regimen fit** | **5%** | Safe complementary use, bundle logic, replenishment and family navigation without confusion or unsafe combinations | Regulatory/clinical review and customer-comprehension evidence |

## Scoring Method

Score each criterion from 0 to 5 and multiply by the assigned weight. Record evidence confidence separately as high, medium or low. A high numerical score based on weak or archival evidence must not outrank a lower score supported by current verified data.

The following are mandatory disqualifiers: unresolved prohibited or disease claim; unclear formula or botanical identity; unacceptable safety concern; no lawful licence or manufacturing route; missing critical warning; untraceable supplier; no stable production route; or inability to produce an accurate sellable pack.

During pilot, monitor net revenue, gross contribution, average order value, cash conversion, units per store per week, retailer reorder, on-time-in-full delivery, fill rate, stockouts, returns, trial-to-repeat, repeat interval, review rate, complaints, adverse events, qualified traffic, product-view-to-cart and checkout completion. Leadership should set thresholds after current baselines and channel terms are established.

# 18. Conclusion

NMS may possess significant heritage value, portfolio knowledge and historical trade credibility, but the working record is not a safe current corporate, product, regulatory or commercial master. That is not a reason to abandon transformation; it is the reason to sequence it properly.

JB3AI recommends that NMS establish verified corporate and product truth, preserve rather than republish the archive, quarantine unsafe claims, select three to five lower-risk hero products, build a modular and accountable identity, implement governed product and service data, and conduct a controlled Gauteng pilot. Scale should follow only when the evidence shows compliant operation, repeat demand, positive gross contribution, reliable supply, safe customer support and retailer reorder.

The winning brand idea is not nostalgia and not miracle language. It is controlled transparency: customers and partners should be able to see what the product is, how it should be used, what cautions apply, who is accountable and where the information came from. Heritage may open the story; present-day proof must carry it.

The Board is asked to approve the programme direction and immediate validation mandate—not an uncontrolled public launch. The controlling principle remains:

> **Truth before identity. Portfolio before platform. Compliance before promotion. Pilot before scale.**
