# Natural Medicinal Services (NMS): Definitive Source-of-Truth Synthesis

**Prepared for:** NMS leadership and the JB3AI transformation team  
**Status:** Consolidated working source of truth; not legal, regulatory, medical, tax, or investment approval  
**Source base:** 12 structured document audits  
**Control principle:** **Truth before identity. Portfolio before platform. Compliance before promotion. Pilot before scale.**

## Status legend and evidence rule

This synthesis separates what the audited material supports from what remains to be approved.

| Label | Meaning | How it may be used |
|---|---|---|
| **FACT** | Directly supported by the supplied audits, including facts about what legacy records say or contain. A fact that a claim appears in an old document is not proof that the claim itself is true. | Internal planning; public use only where the underlying fact is independently verified and approved. |
| **PROPOSAL** | A recommended strategy, design direction, operating model, target, hypothesis, or implementation sequence. | Subject to validation, responsible-owner review, and approval. |
| **DECISION** | A choice or sign-off still required from authorised NMS owners. | No implementation assumption should be made until recorded. |
| **HOLD** | Information or an asset that must not be published, sold, migrated, or treated as current until verification and approval are complete. | Quarantine in the controlled data room/PIM. |

Where sources conflict, this document does not invent chronology or choose a convenient version. It uses the most complete and operationally controlled material as the working reference, preserves unresolved alternatives in the conflict register, and assigns a resolution action.

## 1. JB3AI introduction

**PROPOSAL.** JB3AI should act as transformation integrator and delivery partner, not as the final authority on NMS corporate identity, product legality, therapeutic claims, manufacturing quality, or pharmacovigilance. Its role is to coordinate discovery, establish the controlled data room and Product Information Management (PIM) process, convert approved strategy into brand and digital systems, manage testing, and report gate evidence to NMS decision-makers.

JB3AI may facilitate these workstreams, but copy, labels, product masters, policies, regulatory positions, quality statements, and launch gates require approval by the relevant NMS owner and specialist.

**FACT.** The audits collectively describe a potentially valuable Johannesburg botanical heritage, a broad historical catalogue, and evidence of past trade infrastructure. They also show that the current operating truth is not reliably established. The correct starting point is therefore validation rather than a rapid cosmetic relaunch.

**PROPOSAL.** JB3AI’s mandate should be framed as follows: preserve the archive; establish corporate and product truth; select a small compliant portfolio; create an accountable brand system; build a controlled, accessible, low-data digital platform; test in Gauteng; and scale only when compliance, service, demand, repeat purchase, contribution, supply, and safety thresholds are met.

## 3. Brand history

### 3.1 What the source base supports

**FACT.** Multiple legacy and strategic audits repeatedly describe NMS as having Johannesburg/South African herbal roots traced to **1934**. However, the audits do not establish an uninterrupted legal succession from 1934 to the present operating entity.

**FACT.** Historical materials describe a broad portfolio spanning more than 150 simplex/raw herbs, herbal remedies, medicinal teas and compounds, topical preparations, hair/scalp/oral products, tissue salts, dietary products, beverages and substitutes, and imported or distributed ranges associated with Hermes, Dr Ernst Richter and Kernosan/Kennosan.

**FACT.** Historical records contain evidence of trade infrastructure: internal product codes, EAN/UPC barcodes, NAPPI fields, case-pack logic, suggested retail pricing, wholesale vendor references, and relationships or references involving Alpha Pharm, Dis-Chem, Transpharm, UPD, Pharmed, Shoprite/Transfarm and other pharmacy/health-shop channels. These are historical records, not proof of current listings or active commercial relationships.

**FACT.** Legacy documents refer to Swiss distribution rights or activity beginning in either **1970** or **1979**, and render a related name as both **Kernosan** and **Kennosan**. This remains unresolved and must not be presented as settled history.

**FACT.** Historical names in the portfolio include NMS/Natural Medicinal Services, Nature’s Own, NU Organic, Lexan, Hermes and other distributed ranges. The current legal ownership, trademark status, and intended hierarchy of these names are not established by the audits.

### 3.2 Public-history rule

**PROPOSAL.** Until documentary evidence and legal succession are approved, the strongest permissible working formulation is **“roots traced to 1934 in Johannesburg”**, subject to legal approval. Do not use “Established 1934,” “since 1934,” or language implying continuous corporate operation unless proved.

**PROPOSAL.** Treat historical brochures, prices, products, Swiss references, retailer names, and heritage devices as archival evidence. Archive status does not authorise current commercial claims, endorsements, availability statements, or use of third-party marks.

**DECISION.** Leadership and legal counsel must approve one of three heritage treatments: (1) verified “Established 1934”; (2) qualified “Roots traced to 1934”; or (3) no date claim until evidence is sufficient.

## 4. Current state

### 4.1 Corporate and operational truth

**FACT.** The audited legacy records are not a dependable current operating record. They conflict on legal entity, registration number, trading name, ownership, acquisition wording, addresses, telephone numbers, email addresses, domains, Swiss history, product codes, product names, pricing, VAT calculations, availability, returns, ecommerce terms, and safety messaging.

**FACT.** Candidate registration references include **2007/017961/07**, **1980/008685/07**, and an incomplete 2011 number. Candidate legal/trading names include “Natural Medicinal Service,” “Natural Medicinal Services (Pty) Ltd,” and “Natural Medicinal Services TVL. (Pty) Ltd.” Ownership is rendered as “Mvelo” and “Myelo Pharmaceuticals Group,” with an undated “recent acquisition.” None should be selected as authoritative without CIPC and transaction-record diligence.

**FACT.** Product information is fragmented across legacy PDFs, brochures, price lists, newer files that reproduce older content, and a draft product master. There is no audited evidence in the supplied material of one approved current master covering formulations, specifications, claims, labels, licences, stock, costs, prices, margins, logistics, digital assets, and ownership.

**FACT.** The draft product master generated on 16 August 2026 contains **247 tracked rows**: 27 core historical price-list rows, 71 additional legacy products/variants, and 149 crude-herb lines. Every entry is on **Hold** or “On enquiry—hold until identity, safety and regulatory review.” All eight Packaging & QA release checks are “Not started.”

**FACT.** Historical prices in the draft master originate from a price list effective 1 May 2017. They are archival, not current prices. Some newer file metadata appears to date from 2026 while materially reproducing 2017 catalogue content and unresolved errors; file metadata must not be used as evidence that the commercial data is current.

### 4.2 Brand and digital state

**FACT.** The brand architecture is unresolved across NMS, Nature’s Own, NU Organic, Lexan and distributed brands. “Nature’s Own” may possess recognition, but its ownership, protectability, apostrophe treatment, customer meaning, and risk of implying “natural equals harmless” have not been validated.

**FACT.** Ten standard asset classes are specified in a manifest—primary wordmark, wordmark-only, boxed monogram, micro monogram, quality endorsement, corporate endorsement, social avatar, email signature, document header, and a 24-unit information-icon system—but all are marked **“To be designed.”**

**FACT.** An “NMS Standard” token file exists at version 0.9 with status **“challenger-concept.”** It is a design proposal, not an approved brand standard.

**FACT.** Legacy website/shop terms are stale or incomplete, including contradictory return windows. The audits do not prove readiness in product compliance, live stock, current price/VAT, fulfilment, payments, courier terms, returns, privacy, adverse-event handling, or customer support.

### 4.3 Claims and quality state

**FACT.** Legacy content contains direct or implied claims involving disease treatment, cure, detoxification, blood pressure, diabetes, infection, pregnancy, slimming and other high-risk outcomes. Some legacy material also acknowledges toxicity, carcinogenicity, interactions, and that no herb can be said to cure an ailment. This is a material safety and credibility contradiction.

**FACT.** Current manufacturing-licence scope, product classifications, controlled formulas, stability evidence, quality specifications, supplier traceability, and permitted claim sets are not established by the audits.

**HOLD.** No legacy product description, testimonial, price, barcode/NAPPI reference, formula, warning, availability statement, retailer relationship, or historical claim may be migrated directly to the public site or sales materials.

## 5. Future implementation

### 5.1 Reconciled programme structure

The most complete and risk-controlled synthesis is a validation-first programme embedded in an approximately 18-month gated transformation. The shorter “90-day revival” plans are useful as delivery sprints but conflict with the required corporate, product and regulatory validation if interpreted as permission for an immediate public launch.

| Phase | Status | Indicative sequence | Required outputs | Gate |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Mobilise and preserve | **PROPOSAL** | Week 0–1 | Charter, named owners, data-room structure, access controls, archive freeze, issue log, approval workflow | Mobilisation acceptance |
| Corporate, product and compliance truth | **PROPOSAL** | Weeks 1–6 | CIPC/ownership fact sheet, approved legal particulars, licence map, active-product universe, claims red list, manufacturing and quality gap assessment | **Gate A:** entity, facts, licence map and active universe signed off |
| Market and commercial validation | **PROPOSAL** | Weeks 2–10 | Customer/channel research, sales and repeat baseline, SKU economics, supply assessment, hero scorecards, go/revise/stop pack | **Gate B:** 3–5 hero candidates approved |
| Brand, architecture and experience | **PROPOSAL** | Weeks 7–14 | Name/trademark work, architecture, positioning, voice, three identity routes, packaging hierarchy, service blueprint | Strategy approval |
| Identity, packaging and platform build | **PROPOSAL** | Weeks 12–26 | Final identity assets, 3–5 approved hero artworks, PIM/CMS, catalogue, stockist/trade functions, CRM, chatbot, policies, analytics, SOP/UAT evidence | **Gate C:** launch readiness signed off |
| Gauteng pilot | **PROPOSAL** | Approximately months 7–9 / 90–180 days | Controlled release through NMS DTC where ready, one specialist ecommerce partner, and 15–30 independent pharmacies; safety and economics monitoring | **Gate D:** pilot thresholds met or corrective plan agreed |
| Scale what works | **PROPOSAL** | Months 10–18 | KZN and Western Cape expansion, selective chain submissions, range extensions, channel optimisation | Board investment decision |

### 5.2 Gate discipline

**PROPOSAL.** Gate A must require a verified contracting entity, ownership/control statement, legal particulars, licence/manufacturing map, approved company facts, active SKU universe, and authorised spokespeople. No public identity rollout should precede it.

**PROPOSAL.** Gate B must select only three to five hero SKUs using the reconciled eight-factor scorecard in Section 15. A product with an unresolved prohibited claim, formula identity, material safety issue, licence gap, or unreliable supply cannot progress on commercial attractiveness alone.

**PROPOSAL.** Gate C must require complete signed product master records; approved labels, claims, directions and warnings; release-ready stock; fulfilment and returns tests; approved policies; role-based PIM/CMS workflow; WCAG-informed accessibility; analytics and consent tests; chatbot UAT; customer-support and adverse-event escalation; and executive/regulatory/quality approval.

**PROPOSAL.** Gate D must evaluate sell-through, repeat purchase, gross contribution, cash conversion, fill rate, OTIF, stockouts, returns, complaint rate, adverse events, service performance, digital conversion and retailer reorder evidence. Scale must be evidence-led, not calendar-led.

### 5.3 Data room and governance

**PROPOSAL.** Establish six controlled folders: (1) Corporate Legal & Succession; (2) Regulatory & Licensing; (3) IP & Brand; (4) Commercial & Financial; (5) Digital & IT Policies; and (6) Heritage Archive. Add a seventh operational PIM workspace if the platform requires separation from source records.

**PROPOSAL.** Each approved record should carry an owner, approver, effective date, version, source evidence, review date and superseded-version link. The public website, chatbot, CRM scripts, social library, packaging and trade sheets should consume only approved records.

## 6. New brand and rationale

### 6.1 Strategic position

**PROPOSAL.** Use **“Johannesburg herbal heritage, made accountable”** as the internal strategic positioning line, not automatically as final consumer copy. It reconciles the heritage opportunity with the need for modern proof, while avoiding miracle language.

**PROPOSAL.** The brand promise should be **controlled transparency**: botanical self-care that is inspectable, understandable and responsible. Evidence of accountability should include named common and botanical ingredients, plant part where relevant, controlled manufacturing, batch/lot traceability, clear dosage form and directions, warnings and interaction guidance, quality ownership, accessible escalation, and claims that do not exceed accepted evidence or approved information.

**PROPOSAL.** Voice should be calm, plain, precise, respectful and non-alarmist. Avoid superlatives, exclamation-heavy copy, mystical certainty, implied clinical endorsement, cure language, “detox” shorthand, and any suggestion that natural products are inherently harmless or interchangeable with prescribed treatment.

### 6.2 Architecture

**PROPOSAL.** Adopt a **wordmark-led endorsed masterbrand hypothesis** pending legal and customer validation:

1. **NMS / Natural Medicinal Services:** corporate authority and accountable quality owner.
2. **NMS Quality Mark:** endorsement used only where manufacturing/quality ownership and legal wording are approved.
3. **Consumer-facing range:** Nature’s Own only if trademark, ownership and comprehension testing succeed; otherwise a replacement name.
4. **Need-state family:** navigation such as Daily Balance, Digestive Comfort, Calm & Rest, Seasonal Support, Mobility and Topical Care—only where compliant and useful.
5. **Product descriptor:** unambiguous product name, dosage form, botanical identity, quantity, directions and warnings.
6. **Distributed brands:** visually and legally separated, with roles and ownership made explicit.

**DECISION.** Approve NMS as the corporate masterbrand, decide whether a consumer sub-brand is required, and determine whether Nature’s Own is retainable.

### 6.3 Rationale

**FACT.** The legacy range is too broad and contradictory to function as a coherent current brand. A modular architecture prevents unverified heritage or a vulnerable consumer name from being embedded irreversibly in every asset.

**PROPOSAL.** Make the 1934 module detachable. Make the consumer-name layer replaceable. Keep product identification and safety hierarchy independent of the size or form of the corporate mark. This reduces legal, packaging and migration risk.

## 7. Marketing, website, and social

### 7.1 Marketing role and audiences

**PROPOSAL.** Marketing should first create trust and safe discovery, not maximise reach. Priority audiences identified across the audits are value-conscious Gauteng household buyers, ingredient-aware online shoppers, adults using herbs alongside prescribed medicines, independent pharmacists and health-shop buyers, and raw-herb/practitioner buyers.

**PROPOSAL.** Initial channel priority should be owned website/SEO, CRM/email, WhatsApp care, independent pharmacies, one specialist ecommerce partner, selected practitioner/trade outreach, and carefully controlled paid search/social. National chains and marketplaces should follow pilot evidence and operational readiness.

**FACT.** The audits cite Meta, Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube and LinkedIn as proposed channels. Suggested allocations and posting frequencies vary between documents and are not approved facts. A formal deck used a planning split of Meta 45%, TikTok 25%, LinkedIn 15%, YouTube 10% and testing 5%; a separate revival blueprint used 45/25/15/15 and elsewhere 60/30/10. These are scenarios, not a mandate.

### 7.2 Website

**PROPOSAL.** Build a mobile-first, low-data, accessible website with a verified catalogue, stockist locator, educational library, trade portal, contact and escalation routes, structured product data, consent-aware analytics, and search. Catalogue-first publication can occur only for products whose public information is approved; checkout is a separate gate.

**PROPOSAL.** Core website acceptance criteria should include:

- verified legal particulars and contact routes;
- one approved source for title, description, ingredients, directions, warnings, price/VAT, stock status and pack data;
- WCAG-informed keyboard access, contrast, readable text, labelled forms, alt text and clear errors;
- responsive performance on low-bandwidth mobile connections;
- canonical URLs and appropriate product/organisation structured data;
- POPIA-consistent consent and data minimisation;
- tested privacy, terms, delivery, returns, complaints, adverse-event and product-quality routes;
- analytics with documented events, funnels, dashboards and retention controls;
- role-based publishing, approvals, version history, review dates and rollback.

### 7.3 Social and content

**PROPOSAL.** Content pillars should be: ingredient literacy; how to read labels; safe use and interaction awareness; provenance and quality processes; product selection; dosage form and regimen guidance; pharmacist/qualified-professional escalation; behind-the-scenes accountability; stockist education; and verified heritage storytelling.

**PROPOSAL.** Every post, influencer brief, ad, landing page, SEO article, email and reply script should reference an approved claim set. User-generated content and testimonials must be moderated; NMS must not repost or amplify prohibited health outcomes merely because a customer said them.

**PROPOSAL.** Start with fewer channels executed consistently. Cadence should be set after capacity testing rather than adopting high-frequency planning examples as commitments. Paid media should begin with controlled experiments, clear attribution windows and stop/reallocate rules tied to qualified traffic, product-view-to-cart, checkout completion where live, repeat behaviour and gross contribution—not vanity reach alone.

### 7.4 Market and performance claims

**FACT.** Strategic audits cite market indicators including 9.0% current-price dietary-supplement growth from 2023 to 2024, an 8.1% forecast CAGR from 2025 to 2029, and South African online retail estimates. These are secondary research references and should be source-checked before external publication.

**HOLD.** U.S.-dollar influencer benchmarks, global platform statistics and SaaS-style service tiers in the generic revival blueprint are not NMS commercial facts and should not drive the NMS business case without localisation and validation.

## 8. Products and rebranding

### 8.1 Portfolio source of truth

**FACT.** The most structured product inventory is the draft product master: 247 tracked rows comprising 27 historical core rows, 71 legacy product/variant rows and 149 crude-herb rows. Its value is control and traceability, not launch approval.

**FACT.** Historical portfolio groups include finished oral remedies; medicinal teas and compounds; raw herbs and botanical simples; topicals; hair/scalp/oral care; nutrition, beverages and substitutes; and tissue salts/imported ranges.

**HOLD.** All catalogue entries remain non-publishable until physical stock, controlled specification, regulatory category, barcode/NAPPI, artwork, commercial data, logistics and digital assets are approved.

### 8.2 SKU triage

**PROPOSAL.** Apply a three-status triage before weighted scoring:

| Status | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Red** | Disease/cure/high-risk claim, unresolved identity or safety concern, licensing gap, unstable/unproved manufacture, or prohibited name/position | Quarantine; do not publish or promote; reformulate, rename, substantiate or retire |
| **Amber** | Plausible product with incomplete evidence, label, claims, supply, economics or data | Remediate and rescore |
| **Green candidate** | Lower-risk everyday-wellness proposition with verified product record, stable supply and viable economics | Eligible for hero-SKU scoring; not automatically approved |

**PROPOSAL.** Select three to five hero products for the pilot. Expand to six to ten only after compliance, repeat purchase, contribution and supply resilience are demonstrated.

### 8.3 Product rebranding rules

**PROPOSAL.** Rebranding must not be a label reskin. Each hero SKU needs a controlled record containing legal product name, brand/range, dosage form, quantity, complete formulation, common and scientific names, plant part, allergen and interaction information, directions, warnings, storage, shelf life, lot/expiry method, legal particulars, regulatory classification, permitted claims, responsible person, barcode/NAPPI status, current cost/price/VAT/margin/MOQ, pack specification, dimensions/mass, fulfilment data, returns disposition, approved photography, metadata and alt text.

**PROPOSAL.** Packaging must prioritise selection safety: product name, dosage form, ingredient identity, quantity, directions and warnings may never be sacrificed to enlarge the logo. Use a systematic hierarchy across cartons, bottles, pouches and trade cases, with production specifications approved before artwork release.

**FACT.** The crude-herb taxonomy contains spelling and botanical-name issues requiring expert cleanup. Examples noted include “Pimpenella/Pimpinella anisum,” “Disocorea/Dioscorea villosa,” and “Alchillae/Achillea millefolium.”

**DECISION.** Approve active products only after current inventory, formula, licence/classification, safety, sales, repeat, margin and supplier records are supplied and signed off.

## 11. SAHPRA, regulation, and compliance

### 11.1 Regulatory baseline

**FACT.** The audits identify SAHPRA, the Consumer Protection Act (CPA), Electronic Communications and Transactions Act (ECTA), Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA), and, in one audit, the Traditional Health Practitioners Act as relevant frameworks. The precise legal application to each product, channel and activity requires specialist determination.

**FACT.** One strategic audit states that SAHPRA identifies Western Herbal Medicine and health supplements within Category D. Product treatment depends on composition, dose, intended use, claims, scheduling, registration, licence scope and label content. This must be confirmed against current requirements by the appointed regulatory owner; this synthesis is not a classification decision.

**PROPOSAL.** No public launch, retailer outreach or checkout activation should occur until the licence/manufacturing audit and product-by-product classification are complete. Advertising and customer guidance must not exceed approved information or accepted evidence.

### 11.2 Claims control

**HOLD.** Quarantine disease-treatment, cure, detoxification, blood-pressure, diabetes, infection, pregnancy, slimming and similar therapeutic/outcome copy from legacy materials. Product names themselves may constitute claims and must be reviewed.

**PROPOSAL.** Establish one approved claims matrix for each SKU: permitted exact wording, prohibited wording, required qualifiers, mandatory warnings, evidence source, approver, approval date, channels allowed and review date. Apply it to packaging, website, paid media, organic social, influencers, email, chatbot, call scripts, trade materials, search metadata and testimonials.

**PROPOSAL.** A Responsible Pharmacist or appropriately authorised regulatory professional should sign off Master SKU sheets, labels, ads, educational copy and scripts. If the legally required role differs by product or licence, legal/regulatory diligence must define the correct accountable person.

### 11.3 Safety, quality and traceability

**PROPOSAL.** Every released product should have controlled formulation and botanical identity, supplier qualification, certificates/specifications, manufacturing and batch records, stability/shelf-life basis, contamination/adulteration controls, storage requirements, release responsibility, complaints and recall routes, and lot-level traceability.

**PROPOSAL.** Indigenous or conservation-sensitive sourcing requires species verification, permits where applicable, sustainability checks, supplier rights, provenance, and benefit-sharing review. Do not use provenance marketing until the evidence chain is complete.

**PROPOSAL.** Customer-facing information must cover directions, dosage, warnings, contraindications, interactions, allergens, pregnancy/breastfeeding cautions where applicable, storage, shelf life, and when to seek professional help. Messaging must respect prescribed medicines and never encourage discontinuation without professional advice.

### 11.4 Adverse events, complaints and records

**PROPOSAL.** Implement SOPs that distinguish general enquiry, order issue, complaint, product-quality complaint, adverse event and urgent safety concern. Capture minimum necessary information, time-stamp it, route it to trained owners, preserve source content, meet reporting obligations, and close the loop. Chatbots and social moderators must be able to escalate immediately.

**PROPOSAL.** POPIA controls should include lawful purpose, minimisation, consent where required, privacy notices, secure access, retention/deletion schedules, processor agreements, data-subject routes and breach handling. Health-related information should receive heightened access control.

### 11.5 Release checklist

**FACT.** The draft master defines eight release checks: physical stock verified; controlled specification approved; regulatory category approved; barcode/NAPPI validated; artwork approved; commercial data approved; logistics approved; and digital assets approved.

**PROPOSAL.** Require all eight to be green, with named owners and evidence links, before a SKU becomes publishable or orderable. “NAPPI present in a historical file” does not prove current validity or reimbursement status.

## 12. Customer engagement across calls, chatbot, email, and social

### 12.1 One service model across channels

**PROPOSAL.** Use a unified CRM case model across calls, call bookings, chatbot, email, web forms, WhatsApp and social direct messages. Every interaction should be classified as enquiry, product guidance, order, return, complaint, product-quality issue, adverse event, privacy request or trade lead, with consent and access controls appropriate to the data.

**PROPOSAL.** Core customer-service measures should include response time, first-contact resolution, escalation time, abandonment, complaint rate, adverse-event handling timeliness, review rate, trial-to-repeat, repeat interval, refund/return rate and customer effort. Commercial conversion must not override safety escalation.

### 12.2 Calls and call booking

**PROPOSAL.** Offer a published customer-care line and online call booking only after the responsible team, hours, scripts, service levels and escalation tree are approved. Product-use or interaction questions should route to a pharmacist/qualified professional; emergencies must be directed to appropriate emergency services rather than handled as ordinary support.

**PROPOSAL.** Agents must use approved product records and cannot diagnose, prescribe, promise outcomes or improvise beyond the claim set. Record consent before storing sensitive information. Call notes should pass to the CRM case so customers do not repeat themselves.

### 12.3 Chatbot

**PROPOSAL.** The chatbot should be educational and service-oriented, trained or retrieved exclusively from approved PIM/knowledge-base records. It may help users find products, explain approved directions/warnings, locate stockists, check order status where authenticated, gather a query and route to a human.

**PROPOSAL.** Hard guardrails must block diagnosis, disease-treatment recommendations, contraindication resolution beyond approved wording, pregnancy assurance, medication-change advice, and invented availability or pricing. Low confidence, health-risk keywords, adverse events, complaints, product-quality concerns and privacy requests must trigger full-context human handoff.

**PROPOSAL.** UAT should test accuracy, refusal behaviour, source citation where used, stale-content prevention, prompt injection, personal-data leakage, escalation, multilingual or plain-language comprehension where offered, accessibility and audit logs. The bot should clearly identify itself as automated.

### 12.4 Email and CRM

**PROPOSAL.** Begin with transactional and service email, then approved welcome and education flows, segmentation, replenishment reminders based on real repeat intervals, and consented lifecycle automation. Do not infer medical conditions from clicks or purchases for promotional profiling without specialist privacy review.

**PROPOSAL.** Email claims, subject lines, previews, linked landing pages and automated replies are subject to the same claim matrix as packaging and ads. Include opt-out, sender identity, privacy information and escalation routes.

### 12.5 Social and testimonials

**PROPOSAL.** Publish channel-specific moderation rules. Hide or escalate personal health data posted publicly; do not diagnose in comments; move order issues to authenticated private support; capture adverse events without debating causality publicly; and preserve source records.

**PROPOSAL.** Testimonials require source retention, consent, authenticity checks and compliance review. NMS must not select, edit or amplify an unapproved therapeutic outcome. Influencers require approved briefs, disclosure requirements, prohibited-claims training and pre-publication review where contractually possible.

## 13. Shopify and ordering

### 13.1 Platform status

**FACT.** The audits propose an online store, but do not establish that Shopify has been selected, contracted or configured. Shopify is therefore a platform option, not a source fact.

**DECISION.** Select Shopify only after comparing it with other suitable commerce/CMS approaches against PIM integration, ZAR payments, EFT/card requirements, shipping, stock and ERP links, trade needs, POPIA/security, accessibility, analytics, total cost, content governance, low-data performance and support capability.

### 13.2 Gated commerce model

**PROPOSAL.** Stage 1 should be a verified catalogue, educational site, stockist locator, trade enquiry/portal and customer-care routes. Stage 2 may enable checkout for approved SKUs only after operational acceptance. V1 should avoid international checkout and unrestricted public user-generated claims unless separately reviewed and supported.

**PROPOSAL.** Shopify, if selected, should not become the product source of truth. Approved product data should originate in the controlled PIM/master and be published through governed integration. Manual edits in the storefront should be restricted and reconciled.

### 13.3 Ordering acceptance criteria

**PROPOSAL.** Before checkout activation, confirm and test:

- approved sellable SKU, current stock and allocation rules;
- current ZAR price, VAT treatment, discounts and promotion approval;
- card/EFT or other approved payment methods, fraud controls and reconciliation;
- guest checkout and account privacy choices;
- delivery areas, rates, courier service levels, tracking and failed-delivery handling;
- legal terms, privacy notice, consent, returns/refunds and cancellation rules;
- customer-service hours, order status, complaint and adverse-event routes;
- batch/lot linkage where needed for traceability and recalls;
- confirmation emails/SMS/WhatsApp content and data-sharing controls;
- accessible mobile cart, checkout and error recovery;
- analytics, consent, security, backup, permissions and incident response;
- end-to-end test orders, refunds, partial fulfilment, stockouts, failed payments and returns.

**PROPOSAL.** Retail subscriptions, bundles and broad catalogue migration should follow proof of stable fulfilment, repeat behaviour and regimen suitability. Avoid subscriptions where product use should not be assumed indefinitely or where safety review is needed.

## 15. Product evaluation criteria

### 15.1 Reconciled weighted scorecard

The eight-factor scorecard is the most complete model in the source base and totals 100%. It should supersede narrower five-factor variants for portfolio comparison, while the detailed QA release checklist remains a mandatory pass/fail layer.

| Criterion | Weight | What to test | Minimum evidence |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Regulatory & claims readiness** | **25%** | Classification, licence fit, formula identity, permitted claims, warnings, label feasibility, evidence quality | Approved master record and claim matrix; no unresolved Red issue |
| **Existing demand / repeat potential** | **15%** | Verified historical/current sales, active enquiries, retailer interest, repurchase interval, problem relevance | Current sales/cohort data where available; research and pilot evidence; archival demand clearly separated |
| **Gross contribution** | **15%** | Net revenue less product cost and direct channel costs; VAT, discounts, payment, pick/pack, delivery subsidy, returns, trade margin and promotions | Current costed bill/pack, approved price architecture and channel economics |
| **Supply & quality resilience** | **15%** | Supplier continuity, botanical identity, specifications, yields, lead time, MOQ, batch consistency, stability, contamination controls, alternate supply | Approved specification, qualified suppliers, quality history and continuity plan |
| **Brand differentiation** | **10%** | Distinctive, credible consumer value without prohibited claims; provenance and format advantage | Competitor review, comprehension/trust testing, defensible product story |
| **Retail operability** | **10%** | Shelf clarity, pack size, barcode/NAPPI status, case pack, shelf life, margin, OTIF, replenishment, returns, training burden | Approved pack/logistics master and buyer-economics test |
| **Digital discoverability** | **5%** | Search demand, clear compliant language, structured data, content potential, mobile comprehension | Keyword/intent research and compliant metadata/content route |
| **Cross-sell / regimen fit** | **5%** | Safe complementary use, bundle logic, replenishment and family navigation without confusing or unsafe combinations | Regulatory/clinical review of combination logic and customer comprehension |

### 15.2 Scoring method

**PROPOSAL.** Score each criterion from 0 to 5, multiply by its weight, and record evidence confidence separately. A high score based on weak evidence must not outrank a modest score supported by verified current data.

**PROPOSAL.** Use these mandatory disqualifiers before ranking: unresolved prohibited/disease claim; unclear formula or botanical identity; unacceptable safety concern; no lawful manufacturing/licence route; missing critical warning; untraceable supplier; no stable production route; or inability to create an accurate sellable pack.

### 15.3 Release and pilot thresholds

**PROPOSAL.** A selected hero remains on Hold until all eight QA release checks pass. During pilot, evaluate net revenue, gross contribution, AOV, cash conversion, units/store/week, retailer reorder rate, OTIF, fill rate, stockouts, returns, trial-to-repeat, repeat interval, review rate, complaint/adverse-event rate, qualified traffic, product-view-to-cart and checkout completion.

**DECISION.** Leadership must approve numeric thresholds only after baseline economics and channel agreements are known. The audits name the measures but do not provide reliable NMS-specific thresholds.

## 16. Conclusion

**FACT.** NMS has potentially valuable heritage, portfolio breadth and historical trade infrastructure, but the supplied legacy material cannot safely serve as a current corporate, regulatory, product or commercial record.

**PROPOSAL.** The defensible path is not to revive the full catalogue or rush a 90-day public launch. It is to establish verified corporate and product truth, quarantine unsafe claims, select three to five lower-risk hero SKUs, create a modular accountable identity, build a governed catalogue and service platform, conduct a controlled Gauteng pilot, and scale only what proves safe, repeatable, contributive and operationally resilient.

**PROPOSAL.** The brand should compete through controlled transparency rather than nostalgia or miracle claims. Heritage may open the story; present-day proof must carry it.

**DECISION.** No public launch brief is complete until leadership resolves the legal entity, ownership, heritage wording, active portfolio, regulatory/manufacturing position, brand architecture, ecommerce gate, programme ownership and budget envelope.

## 18. Three theme directions

### Theme 1 — Heritage Apothecary

**PROPOSAL.** A restrained heritage-led direction using deep forest, sage, warm copper/clay and warm parchment, with a controlled serif for verified editorial history and a clear sans serif for product information. Archival assets may appear as evidence-backed storytelling, never as faux nostalgia.

**Strength:** Emotional warmth and continuity.  
**Risk:** Can overstate 1934 continuity, look old-fashioned, or fall into apothecary clichés.  
**Conditions to advance:** Heritage evidence approved; date remains detachable; high legibility; no ornamental clutter, crests or implied medical authority.

### Theme 2 — Modern Botanical — recommended strategic starting point

**PROPOSAL.** A balanced system using Deep Canopy/Forest, Mineral Teal or Sage, Warm Bone/Cream, Carbon and controlled Johannesburg Clay. Use clean, highly legible typography, structured botanical imagery, named ingredients and transparent information hierarchy.

**Strength:** Best balance of natural origin, approachability, pharmacy credibility and mobile/retail performance.  
**Risk:** Can become generic “green wellness” without a distinctive mark and information system.  
**Conditions to advance:** Distinctiveness testing, compliance-neutral Verified Root or challenger symbol, and disciplined product-family architecture.

### Theme 3 — Clinical Nature / NMS Standard

**PROPOSAL.** A rigorous information-first direction built around the NMS Standard wordmark/monogram, strong grids, Public Sans or equivalent, Deep Canopy or navy-like authority, white/warm bone, mineral accents and square geometry. Quality records, COAs, batch information and selection clarity are prominent.

**Strength:** Accountability, digital clarity, production versatility and B2B credibility.  
**Risk:** May feel sterile, over-medical or imply clinical/regulatory status if not carefully worded.  
**Conditions to advance:** Consumer warmth testing, compliance-neutrality pass, and prohibition on medical-cross/shield/checkmark cues.

**DECISION.** Commission and test all three in black and white before colour preference influences judgement. Select only after shelf, mobile, packaging, technical-document, avatar, monochrome, accessibility and audience-comprehension tests.
