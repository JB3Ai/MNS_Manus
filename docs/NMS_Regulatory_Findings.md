# Verified Regulatory and Compliance Findings for NMS

## SAHPRA complementary medicines

SAHPRA states that Category D complementary medicines include discipline-specific medicines and health supplements. Health supplements are limited to low-risk indications and substances, dosage ranges and indications set out in SAHPRA guidance. Continued sale is tied to applicable registration call-up requirements, relevant section 22C licensing, compliance with section 20 and regulations 10, 11, 12 and 42, and other relevant provisions. Low-risk indication framing includes general health enhancement without reference to specific diseases, health maintenance, and relief of minor symptoms not related to a disease or disorder.

The current guideline library lists the core complementary-medicine documents: specified substances (7.06), ZA-CTD quality (7.05), health supplement safety and efficacy (7.04), ZA-CTD application format (7.03), roadmap (7.02), and discipline-specific safety and efficacy (7.01).

The health-supplement safety and efficacy guidance includes allowable claim wording, ingredient-specific minimum/maximum dose ranges, warnings, single- versus multiple-substance formulation treatment, and safety/efficacy requirements. Product claims should therefore be treated as controlled content tied to formulation, daily dose, target population, evidence and mandatory warnings—not as general marketing copy.

The quality guideline expects a structured quality dossier covering administrative information, CTD summaries and Module 3 quality evidence. It includes specifications, analytical procedures and validation, stability-indicating methods where relevant, batch analysis, certificates of analysis, manufacturing-site information, packaging, labelling and import-release controls. At least two final-product certificates of analysis are described for demonstrating batch-release compliance; imported batches have additional local release expectations unless exempted.

## Marketing and customer engagement

The Information Regulator's 3 December 2024 POPIA direct-marketing guidance treats telephone, email, SMS, push notifications, Instagram or LinkedIn direct messages, and cookies as electronic direct-marketing channels. For a non-customer, consent is required before marketing; a person may be approached only once to request consent if they have not previously withheld it. Existing-customer marketing is limited to the responsible party's own similar products or services where details were collected in the context of a sale, with free and simple objection opportunities at collection and in each communication. Objections must stop future marketing, and an objection/suppression database should be maintained.

## E-commerce and consumer protection

The Consumer Protection Act aims to improve consumer information, prohibit unfair marketing and business practices, and create a consistent framework for consumer transactions. The Electronic Communications and Transactions Act regulates and facilitates electronic communications and transactions. The Shopify implementation should therefore include clear seller identity and contact details, product and price disclosure, delivery and returns information, transaction records, consent and privacy controls, and marketing claims that remain within the product's approved/compliant evidence envelope.

## Operational implication

The website and store should use a claims-governance workflow: every product must have a regulatory classification, responsible licence-holder/supplier record, formulation and dose check, approved claim library, mandatory warnings, evidence links, label approval state, quality/stability/batch documentation, and marketing-channel approval status. Products should not be launched merely because creative and inventory are ready.
